    The problem

    Design errors were found too late

    Manual

    Splitter counts, port capacity, cable direction, naming and home counts were all checked by eye after the design was drawn.

    Late

    Errors came back after the design was considered done, so the designer had to reopen it and rework it.

    Inconsistent

    Two people could pass different designs. The rules lived in people's heads and a long standard document, not in the designer's workflow.

    Workflow

    From drawing to hand-off

    1. Import

      Open the base drawing from DWG or DXF.

    2. Design

      Place splices, splitters, terminals and cables; draw service-area boundaries. Works with touch on a tablet.

    3. Validate

      Run QC at any point while designing and get a suggested fix for every issue.

    4. Rename

      Apply the naming convention to every element in one step.

    5. Assign fibers

      Fibers and splitter ports are assigned automatically, and the full connectivity is shown in the straight-line diagram.

    6. Hand off

      Hand over with confidence: export the QC report, BOM and DWG for the design record.

    Built-in documentation

    Every design comes with its own QC report

    The report is produced from the same checks, so documentation is done the moment QC passes. Drafters and project leads get one consistent record of what was designed and what was checked.

    • QC summaryCable direction, splitter structure and capacity, with every critical error and warning and its suggested fix
    • Bill of materialsSplices, splitters by size, terminals by type, tail lengths and cable footage
    • PON size checkArchitecture, allowed home-count range and port utilization for each service area
    • Terminal listEvery terminal with its address count, splitter name and tail length

    See it working

      What it checks

      The design standard, turned into rules

      Each check raises a Critical error or a Warning, and warnings that are acceptable for a design can be marked as accepted.

      Splitter structure & capacity

      Validates the 1st, 2nd and 3rd stage splitters in each service area, and that every 2nd-stage splitter feeds at least one downstream splitter.

      OS0006 (1x4, 2nd stage): no 3rd-stage splitter connected

      Home count per service area

      Adds up homes from every terminal inside each drawn service-area boundary and checks the total against the allowed range for the PON port.

      Service area A: 120 homes, allowed 57–112 on a 1x128 PON

      Cable direction

      Traces every fiber cable from the OLT outwards and flags cables drawn input-to-output the wrong way round.

      Cable SP0002 → SP0001 runs upstream

      Port reserve

      A terminal with a monitoring cable must keep one spare splitter port, so homes + 1 has to fit the port count.

      NP0003: 8 homes + monitoring on 8 ports

      Power & monitoring

      A new OLT needs its power supply, power cable and monitoring cable. An existing, already-powered OLT must not get a second set.

      New OLT has no power supply placed

      Naming & numbering

      Auto-renames splices, terminals, splitters and cables to the naming convention, with no duplicates inside a service area.

      SP0001 · NP0001 · OS0001 · .01.0001
      Build & roadmap

      One designer, end to end

      I turned five years of FTTH design and QA/QC experience into the rule set, then built and tested the tool myself using AI-assisted development. It is published as a web app, so it opens in any browser with nothing to install.

      The aim is to put the full design standard in the designer's hands, so every design is right first time and goes out with confidence.

      Next on the roadmap

      Two-stage splitter support

      The tool validates three-stage designs today. Next, I am adding two-stage architecture rules so the same checks, fiber assignment and reports work for both. The 2-stage view in the illustration at the top previews that direction.

      Alongside it: continued tuning of QC accuracy and smarter splitter placement suggestions.
